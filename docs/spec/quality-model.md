# Quality model

The normative definition of pass 1 of [scoring.md](scoring.md), the
`QualityScore`: what a soul's quality score and tier are, exactly, in parameter
set `yata-quality-v2`. The decisions are ADR-0024, ADR-0025, which moved the SSR
edge, and ADR-0027, which set the v2 catalogue. The derivation, the rejected
alternatives, and the numbers behind every threshold are in the paper,
`papers/quality-model-v2/paper.md`. The structural claims are proved in
`formal/lean/`, and every number on this page is generated by
`formal/calibration/` (ADR-0023).

Nothing on this page is _implemented_ in `yata-core` yet. It is _designed_, with
_tested_ reference computations: the calibration program and the Lean proofs.

## Domain

The model scores **six-star souls** (C). The value tables and the roll rules are
sourced for six stars only (A); a soul below six stars has no quality score, and
the application says so instead of showing a number.

## Features

For each sub-attribute `a` of a soul, its value in **roll units** (C):

```text
e_a = S(a) / hi(a)          and e_a = 0 when a ∉ dom S
```

`hi(a)` is the largest single increment at six stars
([soul-mechanics.md](soul-mechanics.md#values)). `S(a)` is the stored value,
rounded to 10⁻⁶ display units before division, which is the tolerance
soul-mechanics uses for every comparison. One increment is between 4/5 and 1
roll unit (A), so a line with `h` increments has `4h/5 ≤ e_a ≤ h`.

## The catalogue

Quality is judged against a published catalogue of **archetypes** (C, provenance
_authored_). Each values its own set `A_p` of sub-attributes with weight 1,
accepts the main attributes listed per slot, and may carry an **eligibility
predicate** on the soul. Slots 1, 3 and 5 have a fixed main attribute, which
every archetype accepts.

| Archetype      | Useful sub-attributes                      | Eligible when | Slot 2 mains                   | Slot 4 mains                                     | Slot 6 mains                  |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------ | ------------- | ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------- |
| `output` 输出  | `AtkPercent` `Crit` `CritDmg` `Spd`        | always        | `Spd` `AtkPercent`             | `AtkPercent`                                     | `Crit` `CritDmg` `AtkPercent` |
| `hit` 命中     | `EffectHit` `Spd` `HpPercent` `DefPercent` | always        | `Spd` `HpPercent` `DefPercent` | `EffectHit` `HpPercent` `DefPercent`             | `HpPercent` `DefPercent`      |
| `healing` 治疗 | `HpPercent` `Crit` `CritDmg` `Spd`         | always        | `Spd` `HpPercent`              | `HpPercent`                                      | `Crit` `CritDmg` `HpPercent`  |
| `speed` 速度   | `Spd`                                      | `e_Spd ≥ 4`   | `Spd` `HpPercent` `DefPercent` | `EffectHit` `EffectRes` `HpPercent` `DefPercent` | `HpPercent` `DefPercent`      |

What each archetype reads, in words:

- `output` evaluates general offensive sub-attribute quality. Its set is the
  analysis's four core output attributes (Hu 2026, § 4.1).
- `hit` evaluates general Effect-Hit and control-oriented quality.
- `healing` evaluates HP, Crit, Crit DMG and Speed quality, the pattern of
  HP-based healers that can crit. It is `output` with `AtkPercent` and
  `HpPercent` exchanged (B, `healing_is_output_relabelled`). It is a general
  pattern, not a prescription for any Shikigami.
- `speed` evaluates exceptional pure-Speed quality, and is considered only once
  the Speed line reaches four roll units, 12 displayed Speed (C). The gate reads
  the line's value, never how many increments it took. It is Yata's design
  choice, not a game rule.

The archetypes are not exclusive categories: a soul may be judged by several,
and the best result is its quality. `hit`, `healing` and `speed` are authored.
The flat attributes and `EffectRes` are useful to none. EffectRes matters for
particular Shikigami, sets and needs, which pass 2 judges (ADR-0027).

**Candidates.** An archetype is a _candidate_ for a soul when it accepts the
soul's main attribute and its eligibility predicate holds (C). Every legal main
attribute of every slot is accepted by some archetype (B, `Arch.coverage`). A
legal soul has a candidate unless its main is `EffectRes` on slot 4 and
`e_Spd < 4` (B, `candidate_exists_iff`); such a soul is **unrated** in pass 1
(C): it has no score and no tier, and the application says so. Below the gate
`speed` is not a candidate; it is not scored low (B, `speed_not_candidate`).

## Utility

```text
U_p(s) = Σ_{a ∈ A_p} e_a          U_speed(s) = e_Spd
```

- For the four-attribute archetypes `0 ≤ U_p(s) ≤ 9` for every legal soul, and 9
  is reached (B, `Archetype.utility_le_nine`, `paperSoul_utility`): six
  increments at maximum on one useful line and one on each of the other three.
  That is the analysis's theoretical output soul (Hu 2026, § 4.4), for `output`.
- For `speed`, `0 ≤ U_speed(s) ≤ 6` (B, `speed_utility_bounds`): one line holds
  one initial increment and five rolls.
- The main attribute contributes nothing to `U`; it decides only which
  archetypes may judge the soul (C). A Speed main does not count toward
  `U_speed`.

## Normalization

```text
g_p(u) = max(0, 50·u/E_p)                         for u ≤ E_p
g_p(u) = min(100, 50 + 50·(u − E_p)/(M_p − E_p))  for u > E_p

E_p = μ · E[K_p]    M_p = 9 (M_speed = 6)    μ = 9/10
```

`K_p` is the number of increments a +15 soul puts on the archetype's useful
attributes, under the **reference measure** (A, C): the official notice's class
weights 攻击类 36%, 防御类 36%, 功能类 28%, shared equally within a class; an
initial count of 2, 3 or 4 with 1/3 each; initial sub-attributes drawn one by
one by weight without repeats; the rolls of
[soul-mechanics.md](soul-mechanics.md#roll-distribution).

- **0** is a soul with nothing useful, **50** the expected +15 soul of the
  archetype, **100** its attainable maximum (B, `g_zero`, `g_E`, `g_M`). Each
  archetype has its own anchors: cross-archetype comparability comes from them,
  not from every archetype holding the same number of useful attributes (C).
- `g` is bounded in [0, 100], nondecreasing, and strictly increasing on [0, M]
  (B, `g_nonneg`, `g_le_100`, `g_mono`, `g_strict`). Scaling the utility and
  both anchors together leaves it unchanged (B, `g_scale`).
- **`E_p` is exact** (D): the calibration propagates the reference measure over
  every hit count with rational probabilities. `E_p` differs slightly between
  archetypes, because 功能类 attributes weigh 28/3% each and the others 9%:
  `hit` holds two 功能类 attributes, `output` and `healing` one. `output` and
  `healing` have equal `E_p`, because `AtkPercent` and `HpPercent` both weigh 9%
  (D, asserted; B, `healing_expected_eq_output`). The values are in the table
  below and, as exact fractions, in `formal/calibration/out/constants.json`.
- **`E_speed` is unconditional** (C): the expectation over all +15 souls, not
  over the souls that pass `speed`'s gate. The gate decides when the Speed
  reading is shown; the normalization describes the Speed dimension. A reference
  conditioned on the gate would score most eligible souls below 50 (D, in the
  table under "Speed").
- **μ is the mean of one increment in roll units** (C): the midpoint of [4/5,
  1]. The law of an increment is open (A); `E_p`, and so every score, depends on
  it only through its mean (B: `U_p` is a sum of increments). The official
  site's averages give 0.907–0.913; the paper's sensitivity table gives the
  effect of such a mean on the test vectors.

## Quality

For a soul `s` in slot `k` with main `m`, over its candidate archetypes `p` (C):

```text
score_p(s) = g_p(U_p(s))
tier_p(s)  = the tier of s under p, below
quality(s) = the best (tier_p, score_p): higher tier first, then higher score;
             a tie keeps catalogue order; unrated when there is no candidate
best-fit   = the archetype that gave quality(s)
```

A speed-eligible soul can still be best fit as `output`, `hit` or `healing` when
that result is higher; the gate makes `speed` a candidate, not the winner. The
score is displayed to one decimal; tiers use the exact value.

**Components shown with the total.** Only the total is a mathematical component;
the others explain it and are never added into it:

| Field       | Definition, under the best-fit archetype                                                                                                                   |
| ----------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `total`     | `score_p(s)`                                                                                                                                               |
| `archetype` | the best-fit archetype, and `score_p` under every candidate                                                                                                |
| `depth`     | `100 · max_{a ∈ A_p} e_a / 6`: the deepest useful line against its maximum; for `speed`, `100 · e_Spd / 6`                                                 |
| `breadth`   | `100 · (D − 1)/(n − 1)`, `D = U² / Σ e_a²`, 0 when `U = 0`, `n = \|A_p\|`: how many useful lines carry the utility; not applicable to `speed`, and omitted |
| `slot_fit`  | which archetypes accept the main attribute, and which of them are eligible                                                                                 |
| `growth`    | below +15: `g_p(U_p(s) + μ · E[ΔK])`, the score of the expected +15 utility                                                                                |

`depth` counts only useful lines, so concentration on a useless attribute earns
nothing. `breadth` depends only on the shape of the useful values, not their
size, so it does not count the total twice. A one-attribute archetype has no
shape to describe, and `breadth` is omitted rather than shown as 0 or 100.

**Growth** is exact under the reference measure. For a soul with `r = 5 − ⌊ℓ/3⌋`
rolls left and the set `P` of sub-attributes present, the expected number of
further useful increments is `E[ΔK] = f(r, P)`:

```text
f(0, P) = 0
f(r, P) = r · |P ∩ A_p| / 4                                    if |P| = 4
f(r, P) = Σ_a w(a) · ([a ∈ A_p] + f(r − 1, P ∪ {a}))          if |P| < 4
```

with `w(a)` the reference weight of one draw. At +15, `growth` is `total`.
Candidates are decided on the current values: a soul below +15 whose Speed may
later pass the gate is not yet judged by `speed`.

## Tiers

### The four-attribute ladder: `output`, `hit`, `healing`

```text
specialized_p(s)  ⟺  ∃ a ∈ A_p.  e_a > 5

tier_p(s) = UR    if score_p(s) > t_UR,p
          = SP    else if specialized_p(s) and score_p(s) ≥ c_SP,p
          = SSR   else if score_p(s) ≥ c_SSR,p
          = SR    else if score_p(s) ≥ c_SR,p
          = R     else if score_p(s) ≥ c_R,p
          = N     otherwise

u_SR = 5μ = 4.5    u_SSR = 6    u_SP_floor = 7μ = 6.3    u_UR = M − 1 = 8

c_R,p = g_p(E_p) = 50    c_SR,p = g_p(u_SR)    c_SSR,p = g_p(u_SSR)
c_SP,p = g_p(u_SP_floor)    t_UR,p = g_p(u_UR)
```

**Two thresholds, two questions** (C, ADR-0025). The SSR edge asks whether a
soul reaches exceptional overall quality; it is six roll units, the most useful
value one perfect line can hold. The SP floor asks whether an exceptional soul
is strong enough overall to carry the SP mark; it is `7μ`, seven useful
increments at mean value. The milestones are ordered
`u_SR < u_SSR < u_SP_floor < u_UR` (B, `milestones_ordered`).

The band edges are the same **utility milestones** for every four-attribute
archetype; their scores differ between archetypes in the second decimal, because
`E_p` does.

### `speed`

```text
tier_speed(s) = UR    if e_Spd > u_UR,speed = M_speed − 1 = 5
              = SSR   otherwise                     (only when e_Spd ≥ 4)
```

`speed` is tiered only when it is a candidate. It has no N, R or SR, because the
gate lies above them; every eligible soul is at least SSR (B,
`speed_candidate_rank`). Its UR is the same frontier principle as the
four-attribute UR, within one roll unit of the maximum (B, `speed_milestones`),
and a `speed` UR soul put all six increments on Speed (B, `speed_UR_six_hits`).
It has **no SP** (C, ADR-0027): the generic specialization predicate, some
useful line above five roll units, is exactly the UR condition of a one-line
archetype (B, `speed_specialized_iff_UR`), so an SP rule would never fire. The
rule is deterministic and exclusive (B, `speedTier_cases`) and monotone in Speed
(B, `speedTier_mono`).

### What each tier means

In words that match the definitions exactly, on the four-attribute ladder:

- **N**: below the expected soul.
- **R**: at least the expected soul for its archetype.
- **SR**: at least five useful increments' worth at mean value.
- **SSR**: at least six roll units of useful value, the most one useful line can
  hold. SSR begins where one sub-attribute could in theory have received the
  maximum six increments; going beyond it needs useful value outside that one
  line (B, `beyond_six_needs_another_line`). A soul with one perfect useful line
  is at least SSR (B, `perfect_line_at_least_SSR`).
- **SP**: a soul at or above the SP floor, `7μ`, with more than five roll units
  on one useful line. Such a line has taken all five rolls (B,
  `six_hits_of_gt_five`), so SP is decidable from values alone. SP requires both
  the extreme line and the higher floor, so one spectacular line alone stays
  SSR: it reaches `g(6)`, below `c_SP` (B, `single_line_not_SP`,
  `perfect_single_line_is_SSR`). Every SP soul is of SSR quality (B,
  `sp_is_ssr_quality`), and specialization cannot bypass the floor (B,
  `sp_needs_floor`).
- **UR**: within one roll unit of the attainable maximum. Every UR soul has all
  nine increments on useful lines (B, `UR_structure`), and every non-UR soul
  scores strictly below every UR soul under the same archetype (B,
  `UR_score_gt`).

Under `speed`: **SSR** is a Speed line of at least four roll units; **UR** is a
Speed line above five, which only six increments on Speed reach.

Precedence is UR, then SP, then the band (B, `tier_UR_iff`, `tier_SP_iff`,
`tier_cases`). At or above `c_SSR` a soul is SSR, SP or UR; no lower band
appears there (B, `at_ssr_floor_high_tier`). Moving the SSR edge down never
lowers any soul's tier (B, `lower_ssr_floor_never_lowers_tier`).

**The exposed order is tier first, then score** (C). UR is first in it. SP is
above SSR in tier order, but an SSR soul may have a higher score than an SP
soul, by at most `t_UR − c_SP`; SP marks a kind of excellence, not a higher
number. Across archetypes the score thresholds differ: a `speed` UR soul scores
at least `g_speed(5)`, which lies below the four-attribute `t_UR`, and it still
ranks above every SSR and SP soul.

**Monotone under dominance** (B, `score_mono`, `score_strict`,
`tierOn_mono_dominance`, `speed_score_mono`): a soul no worse on any useful
attribute of an archetype has a score and a tier no lower under it, and a
strictly better one scores strictly higher. Raising Speed never removes `speed`
from the candidates (B, `speed_gate_mono`).

**Fair across archetypes.** Each archetype's 50 is its own expected soul, so no
archetype gains because its attributes are drawn less often: a rarer set of
attributes has a lower `E_p`, and the same utility then scores the same distance
above its own expectation. Where the reference measure treats two archetypes
alike, the scales are identical (B, `score_relabel`): `healing` is `output`
under the exchange of `AtkPercent` and `HpPercent`, which share a class, and the
two have the same `E_p` and the same tier rates (D, asserted). `hit` differs
from them in class makeup; the residual difference is reported in the paper.

## Versioning

A score is shown with `yata-quality-v2`. v2 changed the catalogue from `output`,
`hit` and `resist` to `output`, `hit`, `healing` and `speed` (ADR-0027); v1.1
moved the SSR edge from `7μ` to six roll units (ADR-0025). Any change to the
catalogue, μ, the anchors, the thresholds, the eligibility predicates, the
reference measure, or the mechanics they rest on is a new model version. Scores
of different versions are not compared ([scoring.md](scoring.md#parameter-set)).

## Open questions

- The law of one increment within its range (A); the model depends only on its
  mean.
- A reviewed catalogue: the `hit`, `healing` and `speed` sets and their mains
  are authored.
- **Weights.** Every useful attribute has weight 1. The maintainer expects
  `EffectHit` and `Speed` to weigh more in `hit` in a future weighted model;
  calibrating weights is a separate problem, kept out of the catalogue revision.
- Quality below six stars, once the increment ranges are sourced.
